# Individuals and Sole Traders

## Rate scale (residents)

**2024-25 and 2025-26**

| Taxable income | Tax on this income |
| --- | --- |
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16c for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | $4,288 + 30c for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $31,288 + 37c for each $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001+ | $51,638 + 45c for each $1 over $190,000 |

**From 1 July 2026** the 16% rate falls to 15%, and from 1 July 2027 to 14%. Thresholds are unchanged.

**Foreign residents** — no tax-free threshold: 30% to $135,000, then 37% to $190,000, then 45%. No Medicare levy.

**Working holiday makers** (417/462 visas) — 15% to $45,000, then the resident rates above that.

**Minors (Division 6AA)** — a person under 18 at 30 June pays penalty rates on *unearned* ("eligible") income: nil up to $416, 66% of the excess between $417 and $1,307, and 45% of the **whole amount** once it exceeds $1,307. Employment income and income from a deceased estate are "excepted" and taxed at ordinary rates. This is why distributing trust income to children is largely ineffective above $416.

## Medicare levy

2% of taxable income, with a low-income shade-in: no levy below the threshold, then 10c in the dollar until the full 2% applies. Family thresholds increase for each dependent child. Full and half exemptions exist for certain foreign residents, defence force members and people not entitled to Medicare benefits (needs a Medicare Entitlement Statement).

## Medicare levy surcharge

Payable where the taxpayer (and family) had **no private patient hospital cover** and income for surcharge purposes exceeded the base tier. For 2025-26: singles $101,000 / families $202,000, rising through 1%, 1.25% and 1.5% tiers. The family threshold increases by $1,500 for each dependent child after the first.

*Income for MLS purposes* = taxable income + reportable fringe benefits + reportable employer super contributions + net investment losses + exempt foreign employment income (less certain super lump sums).

## Study and training loans

From **1 July 2025** compulsory repayments moved to a **marginal** system: nothing below $67,000, then 15% of income between $67,000 and $125,000 and 17% above $125,000. Before that a flat percentage (1%–10%) applied to the whole of repayment income from $54,435 (2024-25).

*Repayment income* = taxable income + reportable fringe benefits + reportable employer super contributions + net investment losses + exempt foreign employment income. A one-off 20% reduction was applied to outstanding balances as at 1 June 2025.

## Common individual deductions

**Work-related**
- Vehicle: cents per kilometre (88c for 2024-25 and 2025-26, capped at 5,000 business km) or logbook. Home-to-work travel is **not** deductible unless carrying bulky equipment with no secure storage at work, or travelling between workplaces.
- Travel: accommodation, meals and incidentals when travelling for work overnight. A travel diary is required for 6+ consecutive nights away.
- Clothing: only occupation-specific, protective, or a compulsory/registered non-compulsory uniform. Conventional clothing is never deductible.
- Self-education: deductible where it maintains or improves skills in the **current** income-earning activity, or is likely to increase income from it. Not deductible where it opens a new field. The $250 non-deductible threshold was removed from 1 July 2022.
- Working from home: fixed rate per hour (70c for 2024-25 onwards) covering energy, internet, phone, stationery and consumables — a record of **actual hours for the whole year** is required, plus at least one bill for each covered expense. Decline in value of equipment and cleaning are claimed separately. Alternatively use the actual cost method.
- Tools and equipment: immediate deduction under $300 (work-related, not part of a set), otherwise decline in value.
- Union fees, professional association fees, subscriptions, income protection insurance premiums (but **not** life, trauma or TPD in a super fund).

**Investment**
- Interest on borrowings used to acquire income-producing assets.
- Rental property: interest, rates, insurance, repairs (not improvements), agent fees, depreciation on plant, capital works at 2.5%. Note that **travel to inspect a residential rental property is not deductible**, and **second-hand depreciating assets** in residential rental properties acquired after 9 May 2017 generally cannot be depreciated.
- Cost of managing tax affairs, including tax agent fees.

**Never deductible**: fines and penalties, entertainment (unless FBT is paid), most childcare, HELP repayments, the private portion of any expense.

## Key offsets

- **LITO** — up to $700, reducing from $37,500 and cutting out at $66,667.
- **SAPTO** — seniors and pensioners tax offset, transferable between spouses.
- **Private health insurance rebate** — income-tested, claimed as a premium reduction or a refundable offset.
- **Foreign income tax offset (FITO)** — capped by the Australian tax on the doubly-taxed income; a $1,000 de minimis applies without the cap calculation.
- **Zone and overseas forces offsets**, **invalid and carer offset**, **super contributions offsets** (spouse contributions, low income super tax offset, co-contribution).

## Sole traders

A sole trader has no separate legal identity. Business income and deductions go on the business schedule of the individual return, and the individual's marginal rates apply to the whole lot.

Consequences:
- Full personal liability for business debts.
- Business losses can offset other income — **but only if the Division 35 non-commercial loss tests are passed** (adjusted taxable income under $250,000 plus one of: assessable income ≥ $20,000, a profit in 3 of the last 5 years, real property used in the business ≥ $500,000, or other assets ≥ $100,000).
- Cannot employ yourself, pay yourself a wage, or claim a deduction for drawings.
- Super is optional for the owner but personal deductible contributions are available (notice of intent required).
- Eligible for small business concessions: simplified depreciation, the instant asset write-off, simplified trading stock rules, immediate deduction for certain prepaid expenses, the small business income tax offset (16% of the tax on business income, capped at $1,000), and the small business CGT concessions.
- The **PSI rules** are the biggest trap for consultants and contractors — if more than 50% of a contract's income is for personal skills or labour, deductions are restricted and income may be attributed unless the results test or one of the personal services business tests is met.

## Individual PAYG instalments

The ATO enters a taxpayer into the PAYG instalment system once investment or business income produces a notional tax above the entry threshold. Instalments are quarterly (or annual for smaller amounts), calculated either on the instalment rate applied to actual income or on a pre-printed amount. Varying an instalment down below 85% of the eventual liability attracts GIC on the shortfall.
