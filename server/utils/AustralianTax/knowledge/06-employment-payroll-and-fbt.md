# Employment, Payroll and Fringe Benefits Tax

## PAYG withholding

An employer must withhold from salary and wages, directors' fees, termination payments, payments to office holders, and payments under a voluntary agreement or a labour hire arrangement. Amounts withheld are reported through **Single Touch Payroll (STP)** on or before each pay day.

- **STP finalisation** is due **14 July** (31 July for closely held payees of small employers).
- Withholding amounts are remitted with the activity statement — monthly for medium withholders, quarterly for small withholders, and more frequently for large withholders.
- **No ABN withholding** — 47% must be withheld from a payment to a supplier who does not quote an ABN.
- **Failure to withhold** removes the deduction for the payment entirely (s 26-105).

## Superannuation guarantee

| Year | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| 2023-24 | 11% |
| 2024-25 | 11.5% |
| 2025-26 onwards | **12%** |

- Calculated on **ordinary time earnings (OTE)** — includes ordinary hours, over-award payments, most allowances, commissions, bonuses referable to ordinary hours, and paid leave. **Excludes overtime.**
- Capped by the **maximum contribution base** per quarter ($62,500 for 2025-26, so quarterly SG is capped at $7,500).
- Due **28 days after the end of each quarter**. The contribution must be **received by the fund** by that date.
- Late payment triggers the **superannuation guarantee charge (SGC)**: the shortfall calculated on **total salary and wages** (not just OTE), plus nominal interest of 10%, plus an administration fee per employee per quarter. The SGC is **not deductible**, and an SGC statement must be lodged.
- **Contractors** paid wholly or principally for their labour are employees for SG purposes even with an ABN.
- **Payday Super** — legislated to require super to be paid at the same time as salary and wages from 1 July 2026. Confirm the enacted commencement and transitional rules.

## Payroll tax (state)

Payroll tax is a **state and territory** tax, not an ATO tax. Indicative 2025-26 annual thresholds and headline rates:

| Jurisdiction | Threshold | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NSW | $1,200,000 | 5.45% |
| VIC | $1,000,000 | 4.85% (1.2125% regional) |
| QLD | $1,300,000 | 4.75% (4.95% above $6.5m) |
| SA | $1,500,000 | 4.95% |
| WA | $1,000,000 | 5.5% |
| TAS | $1,250,000 | 4.0% (6.1% above $2m) |
| ACT | $2,000,000 | 6.85% |
| NT | $1,500,000 | 5.5% |

Always confirm with the relevant revenue office — these change frequently and several jurisdictions apply surcharges (Victoria's mental health and wellbeing surcharge, Queensland's mental health levy) and threshold phase-outs.

**Grouping** provisions treat related businesses as a single employer sharing one threshold. Taxable wages include superannuation, grossed-up fringe benefits, most contractor payments, bonuses, commissions, allowances and shares or options.

## Fringe benefits tax

The FBT year runs **1 April to 31 March**. The rate is **47%**.

```
FBT payable = (Type 1 taxable value × 2.0802 + Type 2 taxable value × 1.8868) × 47%
```

- **Type 1** — the employer was entitled to a GST credit on the benefit. Grossed up at 2.0802.
- **Type 2** — everything else (GST-free, input taxed, or acquired from an unregistered supplier). Grossed up at 1.8868.

### Categories of fringe benefit

Car, debt waiver, loan, expense payment, housing, living-away-from-home allowance, airline transport, board, meal entertainment, car parking, property, and residual.

### Car fringe benefits

Two methods, and the employer can choose the lower:

- **Statutory formula**: `base value × 20% × (days available ÷ 365) − employee contributions`. The base value is the cost of the car (or its value at the start of a lease), reduced by one third after four full FBT years.
- **Operating cost**: `(total operating costs × private use %) − employee contributions`. Requires a **valid logbook** kept for 12 continuous weeks, valid for 5 years.

**Electric vehicles** — battery electric and hydrogen fuel cell vehicles first held and used on or after 1 July 2022, priced below the luxury car tax fuel-efficient threshold, are **FBT exempt**. The exemption for **plug-in hybrids ended 1 April 2025** unless a pre-existing binding financial commitment continues. Exempt car benefits still count towards **reportable fringe benefits**.

### Common exemptions and reductions

- **Minor benefits** — less than $300 and provided infrequently and irregularly.
- **Otherwise deductible rule** — the taxable value is reduced by the amount the employee could have claimed as a once-only income tax deduction.
- **Work-related items** — a portable electronic device, protective clothing, a briefcase, a tool of trade, or computer software, limited to one similar item per year (small business employers with turnover under $50m can provide multiple).
- **Taxi, ride-share and public transport** travel to or from work, or due to illness.
- **Relocation** benefits, remote area housing, emergency assistance.
- **Car parking** — exempt for small business employers meeting the turnover test where parking is not in a commercial car park.
- **Employee contributions** reduce the taxable value but are assessable income to the employer (and may carry GST).

### Reportable fringe benefits

Where an employee's total taxable value exceeds **$2,000** in the FBT year, the **grossed-up** amount (using the Type 2 factor regardless of the actual type) is reported on their income statement. It is not taxable income but counts for the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayment income, Division 293, child support and most income tests.

### Salary packaging

Effective salary sacrifice requires the agreement to be in place **before** the income is earned. Common packaged items: superannuation (subject to the concessional cap), a novated lease car, portable electronic devices, and — for PBI/health promotion charity/public hospital employees — a capped exempt amount of general living expenses.

### FBT lodgment

The return and payment are due **21 May**, extended to **25 June** where lodged electronically through a registered tax agent. Employers who paid FBT above the instalment threshold in the previous year pay quarterly instalments at label F1/6A on the activity statement.

## Termination payments

- **Employment termination payments (ETPs)** — concessionally taxed up to the ETP cap or the whole-of-income cap (whichever is lower), with the taxable component taxed at 17% (under preservation age: 32%) up to the cap and 47% above it.
- **Genuine redundancy payments** — a tax-free amount comprising a base amount plus a per-completed-year-of-service amount (indexed annually), with the excess treated as an ETP. Only available where the person is under age-pension age at termination.
- **Unused annual and long service leave** — taxed under separate rules depending on when the leave accrued and whether the termination was a genuine redundancy.

## Contractor vs employee

Following *Personnel Contracting* and *Jamsek*, the analysis focuses on the **rights and obligations in the written contract** rather than the subsequent conduct (unless the contract is a sham or has been varied). See TR 2023/4 and PCG 2023/2. Getting this wrong exposes the payer to PAYG withholding, SGC, payroll tax and workers compensation liabilities.
