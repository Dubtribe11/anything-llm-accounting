# Capital Gains Tax and Property

## How CGT works

CGT is not a separate tax — a **net capital gain** is included in assessable income and taxed at the entity's ordinary rate.

```
Capital proceeds
− Cost base
= Capital gain (or capital loss if the proceeds are less than the reduced cost base)

Then, in this order:
  1. Apply current year capital losses
  2. Apply carried-forward capital losses
  3. Apply the CGT discount
  4. Apply the small business CGT concessions
= Net capital gain
```

**Losses are always applied before the discount.** Where there is a choice, apply losses against the *undiscounted* gains first — that preserves the discount on the gains that remain.

## Cost base

The five elements of the cost base:
1. Acquisition cost (money paid plus the market value of any property given)
2. Incidental costs — stamp duty, legal fees, agent commission, valuation, transfer costs
3. Ownership costs — interest, rates, land tax, insurance, maintenance (**only** for assets acquired after 20 August 1991, and only where not otherwise deductible)
4. Capital expenditure to increase or preserve the asset's value
5. Capital expenditure to establish, preserve or defend title

The **reduced cost base** (used for losses) excludes element 3 and is reduced by amounts already deducted, such as capital works deductions.

## The CGT discount

| Entity | Discount |
| --- | --- |
| Individual | 50% |
| Trust | 50% (flows through to beneficiaries who apply their own percentage) |
| Complying superannuation fund | 33⅓% |
| **Company** | **Nil** |

Requires the asset to have been held for at least **12 months** (measured from the day after acquisition to the CGT event). Foreign and temporary residents cannot access the discount for periods after 8 May 2012.

Assets acquired **before 21 September 1999** may use **indexation** instead — indexing the cost base by the CPI factor frozen at the September 1999 quarter — where that produces a better result.

Assets acquired **before 20 September 1985** are **pre-CGT** and generally exempt, but major capital improvements and changes in majority underlying ownership can bring them into the net.

## Main residence exemption

A dwelling is fully exempt where it was the taxpayer's main residence for the whole ownership period, was not used to produce income, and the land is 2 hectares or less.

Key rules:
- **Six-year absence rule (s 118-145)** — a dwelling can continue to be treated as the main residence for up to 6 years while rented out (indefinitely if not producing income). The 6-year period resets each time the taxpayer re-establishes it as their main residence.
- **Home first used to produce income (s 118-192)** — the cost base resets to market value at the date it first produced income.
- **Partial exemption** — apportioned by the number of days it was not the main residence, and by the floor area used for income production.
- **Only one main residence at a time** per family unit (with a 6-month overlap when moving).
- **Foreign residents** — since 1 July 2020, a foreign resident at the time of the CGT event generally gets **no** main residence exemption at all, regardless of how long the property was their home. Limited life-events exceptions apply for absences of 6 years or less.
- **Building or renovating (s 118-150)** — up to 4 years of pre-occupancy treatment where the taxpayer moves in as soon as practicable and stays 3 months.

## Small business CGT concessions (Division 152)

### Basic conditions (Subdiv 152-A)

1. A CGT event happens to a CGT asset, resulting in a gain, **and**
2. The taxpayer is either a **CGT small business entity** (aggregated turnover under **$2 million**) or satisfies the **maximum net asset value test** (net value of CGT assets of the taxpayer and connected/affiliated entities is **$6 million or less**), **and**
3. The asset satisfies the **active asset test** — active for at least half the ownership period (or 7½ years if owned more than 15 years).

For **shares in a company or interests in a trust** there are additional conditions: a CGT concession stakeholder must have a small business participation percentage of at least 20% (the 90% test), and the object entity must satisfy the 80% active asset test.

### The four concessions

| Concession | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **15-year exemption** (152-B) | The **whole gain is disregarded** where the asset was held 15+ years and the individual is 55 or over and retiring, or permanently incapacitated. Applied **first**, before the CGT discount. Proceeds can be contributed to super under the CGT cap. |
| **50% active asset reduction** (152-C) | Halves the remaining gain (after the CGT discount, so an individual can get to 25% of the original gain). Can be declined to preserve room for the retirement exemption. |
| **Retirement exemption** (152-D) | Disregards up to a **$500,000 lifetime limit** per individual. If under 55 at the time of the choice, the amount must be paid into super. |
| **Rollover** (152-E) | Defers the gain until a replacement active asset is acquired within the replacement asset period (one year before to two years after the event). If no replacement is acquired, the gain crystallises. |

### The CGT cap

Amounts from the 15-year exemption and the retirement exemption can be contributed to superannuation under the **CGT cap**, separate from the concessional and non-concessional caps. An election must be given to the fund on or before making the contribution.

## Common CGT events

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| **A1** | Disposal of a CGT asset — the most common. Timing is the **contract date**, not settlement. |
| **C2** | Cancellation, surrender or expiry of an intangible asset |
| **D1** | Creating contractual or other rights (e.g. a restraint of trade payment) |
| **E4** | A non-assessable payment from a trust — reduces the cost base of the units, and produces a gain once the cost base is nil |
| **G1** | A non-assessable payment from a company in respect of shares |
| **I1** | An individual or company ceasing to be an Australian resident — deemed disposal of assets that are not taxable Australian property, with an option to defer |
| **K6** | Pre-CGT shares where post-CGT property makes up more than 75% of the company's net value |

## Foreign resident capital gains withholding

For contracts entered into from **1 January 2025**: **15%** withholding on **all** disposals of taxable Australian real property (the previous $750,000 threshold was removed and the rate rose from 12.5%). The purchaser must withhold and pay the amount to the ATO unless the vendor provides an **ATO clearance certificate**. Australian resident vendors should apply for a clearance certificate well before settlement — processing takes time.

## Property specifics

- **Revenue vs capital** — property held as trading stock (a property developer) produces ordinary income, not a capital gain, and gets no CGT discount. An isolated profit-making transaction can also be ordinary income under *Myer Emporium* / TR 92/3.
- **Capital works deductions reduce the cost base** — a property that has claimed Division 43 deductions has a lower cost base on sale by the amount claimed.
- **Vacant land** — deductions for holding costs on vacant land are denied to individuals and most trusts unless the land is used in a business or an exception applies.
- **Land tax and duty surcharges** apply to foreign persons in most states, and trust deeds that do not exclude foreign beneficiaries are caught.
- **Subdividing the family home** — the exemption applies only to the dwelling and adjacent land; a sold vacant subdivided block is generally taxable.

## Rollovers and restructures

- **Marriage or relationship breakdown rollover** (Subdiv 126-A) — automatic where the transfer is under a court order or binding financial agreement.
- **Deceased estates** — death is not a CGT event for assets passing to a beneficiary or legal personal representative. Pre-CGT assets get a market-value cost base at the date of death; post-CGT assets are inherited at the deceased's cost base. A dwelling that was the deceased's main residence is exempt if disposed of within **2 years** (extendable).
- **Scrip for scrip rollover** (Subdiv 124-M) — for takeovers and mergers.
- **Small business restructure rollover** (Subdiv 328-G) — moves active assets between entities under the same ultimate economic ownership without a tax cost.
- **Trust to company (Div 122-A/122-B)** and **partnership to company** rollovers.
