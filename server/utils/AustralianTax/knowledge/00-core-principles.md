## The income year

The Australian income year runs **1 July to 30 June**. It is written as "2025-26" and often spoken about as "the 2026 year" (the year it ends).

The **FBT year is different** — 1 April to 31 March.

## The basic income tax formula

```
Assessable income  (ordinary income s 6-5 + statutory income s 6-10)
− Deductions       (general s 8-1 + specific)
= Taxable income
× Applicable rate(s)
− Tax offsets (non-refundable first, then refundable)
+ Medicare levy and surcharge
+ Compulsory study/training loan repayment
− PAYG credits
= Amount payable or refundable
```

Note the ordering. Non-refundable offsets (LITO, SAPTO, foreign income tax offset in most cases) can only reduce tax to nil. Refundable offsets (franking credits for individuals, super funds and some others; the refundable R&D tax offset; the private health insurance rebate) can generate a refund.

## Section 8-1 — the general deduction provision

A loss or outgoing is deductible to the extent that it is:
- incurred in **gaining or producing assessable income**, or
- necessarily incurred in **carrying on a business** for that purpose,

**except** to the extent that it is:
- capital or of a capital nature,
- private or domestic,
- incurred in gaining exempt or non-assessable non-exempt income, or
- prevented from being deducted by a specific provision.

The three questions that decide most deduction disputes:
1. Is there a sufficient **nexus** with earning assessable income? (*Lunney*, *Payne*, *Ronpibon Tin*)
2. Is it **capital** rather than revenue? (*Sun Newspapers* — enduring benefit, once-and-for-all, business structure vs. process)
3. Has it been **apportioned** for any private or non-income-producing use?

## Residency

Residency drives everything: rate scale, tax-free threshold, Medicare levy, CGT scope, and the availability of the CGT discount and main residence exemption.

An individual is a **resident for tax purposes** if they satisfy any one of:
1. **Ordinary concepts test** — they reside in Australia (the primary test; the others only matter if this fails)
2. **Domicile test** — Australian domicile unless the Commissioner is satisfied their permanent place of abode is outside Australia
3. **183-day test** — present for more than half the income year, unless usual place of abode is overseas and there is no intention to take up residence
4. **Superannuation test** — a member of a CSS or PSS Commonwealth super scheme

Residents are taxed on **worldwide income**. Foreign residents are taxed on **Australian-sourced income** and on **taxable Australian property** for CGT purposes. Foreign residents get no tax-free threshold, no CGT discount on assets acquired after 8 May 2012, and generally cannot access the main residence exemption.

## Entity comparison at a glance

| | Sole trader | Partnership | Company | Discretionary trust | SMSF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Separate legal entity | No | No | Yes | No (the trustee acts) | No (trustee acts) |
| Taxed at entity level | No — individual rates | No — flows to partners | Yes — 25% or 30% | Only on undistributed income (45%) | Yes — 15% |
| Asset protection | None | None (joint and several) | Strong | Strong | N/A |
| CGT discount | 50% | 50% (at partner level) | **None** | 50% (flows through) | 33⅓% |
| Income splitting | No | By fixed shares | By dividends (fixed to shareholdings) | Discretionary each year | No |
| Losses | Offset other income (subject to Div 35) | Flow to partners | Trapped, COT/BCT to recoup | **Trapped in the trust** | Trapped |
| Setup/running cost | Lowest | Low | Moderate–high | Moderate–high | High |
| Succession | Dies with owner | Dissolves on change | Shares transfer | Deed/appointor control | Member-based |

The single most common structuring point: **a company gets no CGT discount**, so holding appreciating assets in a company is usually poor planning. A trust with a corporate beneficiary ("bucket company") gets both the 50% discount on gains and a 25%/30% cap on retained income.

## Integrity rules to check on every engagement

- **Part IVA** (ITAA 1936) — general anti-avoidance. Applies where a scheme was entered into for the dominant purpose of obtaining a tax benefit.
- **Division 7A** — payments, loans and debt forgiveness by a private company to shareholders/associates are deemed unfranked dividends unless put on complying terms.
- **Section 100A** — trust reimbursement agreements. See PCG 2022/2 (white/green/red zones) and TR 2022/4.
- **Division 35** — non-commercial losses. An individual's business loss is quarantined unless income is under $250,000 **and** one of four tests is met (assessable income ≥ $20,000; profit in 3 of 5 years; real property ≥ $500,000; other assets ≥ $100,000), or the Commissioner exercises discretion.
- **Personal services income (Part 2-42)** — where more than 50% of income from a contract is for a person's skills/labour, income is attributed to the individual unless the results test is met or the entity is a personal services business (80% rule plus unrelated clients / employment / business premises tests).
- **Division 293** — extra 15% on concessional super contributions for individuals with income over $250,000.
- **Thin capitalisation, transfer pricing, hybrid mismatch** — for entities with cross-border dealings.

## Substantiation

- Records must be kept for **5 years** from lodgment (longer where a CGT asset is involved — keep CGT records until 5 years after the CGT event).
- Work-related expense claims over **$300 in total** require written evidence for all of them.
- Car expenses: either the **cents per kilometre** method (max 5,000 business km, no written evidence needed but the basis of the estimate must be shown) or the **logbook** method (12 consecutive weeks, valid 5 years).
- Working from home: the **fixed rate** method (a set rate per hour covering electricity, gas, internet, phone, stationery, computer consumables — with a record of actual hours worked for the whole year) or the **actual cost** method.
- Travel: a travel diary is required for trips of 6 or more consecutive nights away.

## Penalties and interest

- **Failure to lodge on time**: penalty units per 28-day period, multiplied for medium and large entities.
- **Shortfall penalties**: 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (recklessness), 75% (intentional disregard). Reduced for voluntary disclosure.
- **General interest charge (GIC)** on unpaid tax; **shortfall interest charge (SIC)** on amended assessments. GIC is no longer deductible for income years starting on or after 1 July 2025 — confirm the enacted position.
- **Safe harbour**: no shortfall penalty where the taxpayer gave a registered agent all relevant information and the agent's error caused the shortfall.

## Scope of advice

Tax agent services in Australia are regulated under the *Tax Agent Services Act 2009*. Only a registered tax agent or BAS agent may provide tax agent services for a fee. Analysis, calculations and explanations of the law are general information; signing off a return or giving personal tax advice is regulated work.
