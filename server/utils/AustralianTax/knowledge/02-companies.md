# Companies

## Rates

| | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Base rate entity | 25% |
| All other companies | 30% |

A **base rate entity** for an income year is a company that:
1. has **aggregated turnover under $50 million** for that year, **and**
2. has **no more than 80%** of its assessable income as *base rate entity passive income*.

Base rate entity passive income includes: distributions (other than non-portfolio dividends), franking credits on those distributions, non-share dividends, interest, royalties, rent, gains on qualifying securities, net capital gains, and amounts flowing through partnerships and trusts that are referable to any of the above.

A "bucket company" that only ever receives trust distributions of investment income will usually **fail** the passive income test and pay 30%.

Companies pay tax on the **first dollar** — there is no tax-free threshold — and get **no CGT discount**.

## Imputation (franking)

Franking credits pass the company tax already paid through to shareholders so profits are ultimately taxed at the shareholder's rate.

```
Franking credit = cash dividend × (corporate tax rate for imputation purposes ÷ (1 − that rate))
Grossed-up amount = cash dividend + franking credit
```

At 30%: a $7,000 dividend carries a $3,000 credit and grosses up to $10,000.
At 25%: a $7,500 dividend carries a $2,500 credit and grosses up to $10,000.

**The corporate tax rate for imputation purposes** uses the *previous* year's aggregated turnover with the current year's assumptions about passive income. It can differ from the rate the company actually pays, which is how franking account deficits arise when a company's rate changes.

Rules to watch:
- **Benchmark rule** — all frankable distributions in a franking period must be franked to the same percentage.
- **Holding period rule** — shares must be held at risk for 45 days (90 for preference shares) to claim the credits, unless the shareholder's total franking credits for the year are $5,000 or less.
- **Franking deficit tax** — payable if the franking account is in deficit at 30 June; a partially offsettable FDT offset applies, reduced by 30% where the deficit exceeds 10% of credits.
- Excess franking credits are **refundable** to individuals and super funds, but **not** to companies — a company converts them to a tax loss under s 36-55.

## Division 7A

Division 7A treats a payment, loan or debt forgiveness by a **private company** to a **shareholder or their associate** as an unfranked deemed dividend, assessable to the recipient, and limited by the company's distributable surplus.

To avoid a deemed dividend, a loan must be put on complying terms:
- a **written agreement** in place before the company's lodgment day for the year the loan was made,
- interest at least the **benchmark interest rate** (8.77% for 2024-25; 8.37% for 2025-26 — always confirm the published rate),
- a maximum term of **7 years unsecured** or **25 years secured** by a registered mortgage over real property where the loan does not exceed 110% of the property value,
- **minimum yearly repayments** made by 30 June each year.

```
MYR = (amount not repaid at the end of the previous year × benchmark rate)
      ÷ (1 − (1 + benchmark rate)^−remaining term)
```

Other Division 7A points:
- **Section 109R** — a repayment is disregarded if the taxpayer borrowed a similar or larger amount from the company to make it. Paying down a Div 7A loan with a dividend from the same company is fine (the dividend is income, then the cash repays the loan) but a fresh loan is not.
- **Unpaid present entitlements (UPEs)** — a UPE owed by a trust to a corporate beneficiary can be treated as a Division 7A loan. See TD 2022/11. Sub-trust arrangements under the old PS LA 2010/4 have matured; each affected UPE needs a plan.
- **Division 7A and the Commissioner's discretion (s 109RB)** — available for honest mistakes and inadvertent omissions.

## Losses

Company tax losses are trapped in the company. To recoup them the company must satisfy either:
- the **continuity of ownership test (COT)** — more than 50% continuity of voting, dividend and capital rights throughout the ownership test period, or
- the **business continuity test (BCT)** — carrying on the same business, or a *similar* business (Subdiv 165-E, extended by the similar business test for losses made in 2015-16 and later).

Capital losses can only offset capital gains and are subject to the same tests.

## Small business concessions available to companies

- **Instant asset write-off** and simplified depreciation pooling (aggregated turnover under $10m).
- **Simplified trading stock** rules — no stocktake needed where the movement is under $5,000.
- **Immediate deduction for prepaid expenses** with a service period of 12 months or less.
- **Small business CGT concessions** (Division 152) — but note the extra conditions for shares in a company, and that a company cannot access the CGT discount.
- **Small business restructure rollover** (Subdiv 328-G) — allows the transfer of active assets between entities under the same ultimate economic ownership without a CGT or income tax cost.
- **R&D tax incentive** — refundable offset at the company's tax rate plus 18.5% for companies with aggregated turnover under $20m; non-refundable premium of 8.5% or 16.5% depending on R&D intensity for larger companies. Registration with AusIndustry within 10 months of year end is mandatory.

## Directors' obligations

- **Director penalty notices** — directors are personally liable for unpaid PAYG withholding, GST and superannuation guarantee charge. Amounts reported within the lodgment deadline can be remitted by placing the company into administration or liquidation; unreported amounts create a **lockdown** DPN that can only be discharged by payment.
- Directors must hold a **director ID**.
- **Loans to directors** must be considered under both Division 7A and Corporations Act related-party rules.

## Practical structuring notes

- Companies are effective for **retaining and reinvesting profits** at 25%/30%, and for trading businesses that need asset protection.
- They are usually poor holders of **appreciating capital assets** because there is no CGT discount and extracting the gain triggers a second layer of tax.
- A company as a **beneficiary of a discretionary trust** ("bucket company") caps tax on retained trust profits at 25%/30% while the trust keeps the CGT discount on gains — but the UPE must be paid or put on Division 7A terms.
- Dividends must be paid in proportion to shareholdings unless different share classes exist. Dividend access shares and similar arrangements attract Part IVA attention.
